## 1. Bare Euler powers: exact coefficient ideal

Let p be prime, e=p-1, O=Z_p[zeta_p], pi=1-zeta_p, and A=I-U, with U multiplication by zeta_p. Identify the integral primitive cyclotomic lattice Z[zeta_p] with the augmentation lattice in Z^p via the consecutive differences f_i=e_i-e_{i+1}, 0<=i<=p-2. Cyclic shift is orthogonal in the physical slot norm.

The uniformizer identity pi^e O=pO, and O/pO=F_p[pi]/(pi^e), show that for m=ae+b, 0<=b<e, the Smith exponents of A^m are a+1 (b times), a (e-b times). Indeed O/pi^b O is an F_p-vector space of dimension b, then multiply the lattice by p^a. The determinant is p^m.

Every primitive Z_p-linear functional is ell_beta(v)=Tr(beta v), with beta in the inverse different and t=v_pi(beta)+e-1 between 0 and e-1. The different is generated by Phi_p'(zeta_p), of pi-valuation e-1 (from (zeta_p-1)Phi_p'(zeta_p)=p*zeta_p^(p-1)). For any integer q, Tr(beta*pi^m O) subset p^q Z_p iff beta*pi^m O subset p^q D^{-1}: this uses closure of the ideal under multiplication by O. Equivalently m+t>=eq. Thus the exact image ideal is
    ell_beta(A^m O)=p^floor((m+t)/e) Z_p.
Its maximum among primitive tests is p^ceil(m/e), attained by trace (beta=1). The minimum is p^floor(m/e). For 1<=m<p-1, the determinant has m copies of p but no primitive scalar test has more than one forced copy. This is not a statement about a specifically chosen vector with extra divisibility.

## 2. Explicit integral realignment can concentrate all m copies

In the basis 1,zeta,...,zeta^(e-1), let ell=(1,...,1) and u the last coordinate vector. Then ell A=p u^T: the first e-1 columns of A are consecutive differences, and the final column has entries 1,...,1,2. Set
    S=I+u(ell-u^T), B=A S.
Since (ell-u^T)u=0, S is unimodular with inverse I-u(ell-u^T). Hence
    det B=p, ell B=p ell, ell B^m=p^m ell.
In the integral basis of ker(ell) given by the first e-1 coordinates (last coordinate minus their sum), B restricts to a lower triangular matrix with diagonal 1 and subdiagonal -1. The quotient by ker(ell) is multiplied by p. Consequently the Smith form of B^m is diag(1,...,1,p^m), and L/B^mL is Z/p^m Z. This is persistent coefficient multiplicity without adding dimensions.

However the spectrum of B is p once and 1 with multiplicity e-1. In particular tr(B^m)=p^m+e-1; tr(I-B)=1-p instead of tr(U)=-1 (except p=2). B is not a cyclic primitive Euler operator. A commuting integral automorphism of the cyclic lattice is multiplication by a unit (the image of 1 determines it), so inserting such automorphisms leaves the Smith exponents of A^m unchanged. The noncommuting realignment is essential in this example.
