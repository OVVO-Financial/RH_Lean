### E. Mathematical Derivation

#### E1. Exact extraction and upper bound: GL1-GL2

The map (n,d) -> (g,m,k) above is bijective onto the indicated primitive
pairs. If g is squareful, both sides of the proposed product identity
vanish. For squarefree g, any prime shared by g and m or m+k makes the
corresponding original integer squareful. Otherwise multiplicativity
gives mu(gm)mu(g(m+k))=mu(g)^2 mu(m)mu(m+k). This proves (D1) including
the zero cases.

For fixed g there are at most 3X/g+1 integers m in the stated interval.
If q=floor((h-1)/g), then

~~~text
sum_{k=1}^q (h-gk) = qh-gq(q+1)/2 <= h^2/(2g).
~~~

Take absolute values only in the sector being paid, then drop its masks.
Consequently

~~~text
|T_ge| <= W_ge
 <= h^2 sum_{g=G}^{h-1} (3X/g^2+1/g)
 <= h^2 (3X/(G-1) + sum_{g=G}^{h-1} 1/g)
 <= h^2 (3X/(G-1) + log(h/(G-1)))
 <= 8 X h^2/G.                                           (E1)
~~~

The reciprocal-square bound follows by telescoping
1/g^2 <= 1/(g(g-1)); the harmonic bound follows by integrating 1/u
over [G-1,h-1] and enlarging the endpoint to h.
Finally log(h/(G-1))<=h/(G-1)<=X/(G-1) and G/(G-1)<=2.
No estimate for a signed primitive correlation has been inserted here.

#### E2. Genuine-support lower bound: GL3

Choose integer triples in the box

~~~text
G <= g < 2G,       L <= m < 2L,       1 <= k <= K,
g == m == k == 1 (mod Q),            Q=420.
~~~

Let A,B,C be the three progression cardinalities. Since
min(G,L,K)>=10^10>2Q,

~~~text
A >= G/(2Q),       B >= L/(2Q),       C >= K/(2Q).          (E2)
~~~

Call a triple good when g,m,m+k are squarefree and

~~~text
gcd(g,m)=gcd(g,m+k)=gcd(m,k)=1.                            (E3)
~~~

For a good triple, n=gm lies in [X,4X), d=gk<h/2,
gcd(n,n+d)=g>=G, and mu(n)^2 mu(n+d)^2=1. Its weight
2(h-d) is at least h. Distinct good triples yield distinct original
pairs because g is their exact common divisor.

We count good triples with an explicit union bound. For p in {2,3,5,7}
all six bad events are impossible: g,m,k are 1 modulo p, and m+k is
2 modulo p; for p=2, m+k is 2 modulo 4, so it is not divisible by 4.

For a prime p>7, multiplication by Q is invertible modulo p and p^2.
In any one of these progressions a specified residue modulo p^j occurs
at most population/p^j+1 times. The six bad events are p^2|g, p^2|m,
p^2|(m+k), p|(g,m), p|(g,m+k), and p|(m,k). Each has leading normalized
count at most 1/p^2. This is a counting statement, not independence.

For the first three events it suffices to sum up to sqrt(2G),
sqrt(3L), sqrt(3L), respectively; here K<=L/4 since h<=X.
For the fourth and fifth, sum up to U=min(2G,3L).
For the sixth, sum up to K. Thus the total proportion of bad triples
is at most 6 sum_{p>7} p^(-2) + E, where

~~~text
E =
  sqrt(2G)/A + 2 sqrt(3L)/B
  + 2 [ (1/A+1/B)(1+log U) + U/(AB) ]
  + (1/B+1/C)(1+log K) + K/(BC).                         (E4)
~~~

For example, fixing k bounds the fourth or fifth event by
(A/p+1)(B/p+1) choices of (g,m). Fixing g gives
(B/p+1)(C/p+1) for the sixth. Summing their cross errors uses only
sum_{p<=u} 1/p <= 1+log u and the number of such primes <=u.
No prime-distribution theorem is needed.

Since every prime greater than 7 is at least 11,

~~~text
sum_{p>7} p^(-2) <= sum_{n>=11} n^(-2)
                  <= integral_10^infinity u^(-2) du = 1/10.
~~~

Using (E2), U<=3min(G,L), K<=L/4 and t=min(G,L,K) gives

~~~text
E <= 10Q/sqrt(t)
      + 12Q(1+log(3t))/t
      + 28Q^2/t.                                       (E5)
~~~

For clarity, the two square-root terms contribute less than
(2sqrt(2)+4sqrt(3))Q/sqrt(t)<10Q/sqrt(t). The two g-m
harmonic errors contribute at most 8Q(1+log(3t))/t, and the m-k
harmonic error at most 4Q(1+log(3t))/t. The remaining product errors
are at most 24Q^2/t and 4Q^2/t. The functions used in these comparisons
are decreasing in the stated range.

At t0=10^10, log(3t0)<25. One exact check is
e>8/3 and (8/3)^25>3t0. Hence the right side of (E5) is at most

~~~text
2656689 / 62500000 < 1/5.                               (E6)
~~~

This bound decreases for t>=t0. At least (1-3/5-1/5)ABC=ABC/5
triples are good. By (E2), their number is at least

~~~text
G L K / (40 Q^3) = X K / (40 Q^3).
~~~

Since floor(h/(4G))>=h/(8G) in the current range, their total

~~~text
h X K/(40 Q^3) >= X h^2/(320 Q^3 G)
~~~
