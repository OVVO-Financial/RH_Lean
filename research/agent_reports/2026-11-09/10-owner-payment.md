### D. Mathematical Derivation

#### FW1. Exact packet and ownership map

For a prime p<=y write P_(p-1)=product_(q<p) q and define

```text
B_R(p) = -sum_(e|P_(p-1)) mu(e)*fract(X/(p*e)),
Phi(t,z) = #{1<=k<=floor(t): gcd(k,P_z)=1}.
```

Every nonunit divisor has one unique factorization d=p*e with p=P+(d) and e|P_(p-1). Since mu(p*e)=-mu(e), and fract(X)=0 for the d=1 atom,

```text
F_R = sum_(p<R, p prime) B_R(p),
B_R(p) = Phi(X/p,p-1) - (X/p)*delta(p-1).       (1)
```

Proof of the second equality: expand fract(t)=t-floor(t), and apply finite divisor inclusion-exclusion to the entire e sum. For each integer k, sum_(e|P_(p-1),e|k)mu(e) is exactly 1 if gcd(k,P_(p-1))=1 and 0 otherwise. Thus multiplication by p identifies the counted k with physical n<=X whose least prime is p. Each physical n is assigned once; no historical occurrence is cloned into independent copies.

Crucially, e>X/p remains in the fractional and reciprocal sums. Its two contributions cancel individually:

```text
-mu(e)*fract(X/(pe)) + mu(e)*X/(pe) = 0,  if pe>X.
```

For pe=X the floor is one and MUST remain. The cofactor k=1 contributes the physical prime n=p; k=p contributes n=p^2. Squareful physical integers are not discarded merely because the divisor coefficients are squarefree. The prime R itself is not an owner, but can be an admitted cofactor in another owner's packet.

The finite Euler identity also yields

```text
sum_(p<R) delta(p-1)/p = 1-delta(y),
Z_R = li(X)+1-X+sum_(p<R) Phi(X/p,p-1)-pi(y).   (2)
```

Equation (2) is full bookkeeping, not a new bound. It recovers the original source with its unit correction.

If p<R and p^3>X, every counted k other than 1 must be prime: a composite k with no prime factor below p would satisfy k>=p^2>X/p. Since p^2<=X,

```text
Phi(X/p,p-1) = 1+pi(floor(X/p))-pi(p-1).         (3)
```

For R>=8 this holds for every owner R/2<=p<R; for R>=64 it holds for every owner R/4<=p<R. The strict cubic guard is essential.

There is an exact cross-owner historical triangle. For fixed alpha in (0,1), when all admitted p in [alpha R,R) satisfy the cubic guard, let n_R(alpha)=#{prime p:alpha R<=p<R}. Then

```text
sum_(alpha R<=p<R) Phi(X/p,p-1)
  = n_R(alpha)*(n_R(alpha)+3)/2 + N_cross(R,alpha),
N_cross(R,alpha)
  = #{prime pairs (p,q): alpha R<=p<R<=q, p*q<=X}.       (4)
```

There are n cofactor-one atoms, n prime squares, and n(n-1)/2 pairs of distinct historical primes, assigned to their smaller prime. All their products are <=(R-1)^2<X. The remaining pairs have q>=R. This retains all historical pair multiplicities and the p=q contribution; it is not a sum of independent owner squares.

#### FW3. Opposite proportional-band biases

Fix real constants 0<a<b<=1 BEFORE R and define

```text
B_R[a,b) = sum_(prime p, aR<=p<bR) B_R(p),
T_R = R^2/log^2 R.
```

Then, along the integers R tending to infinity,

```text
B_R[a,b)/T_R -> c(a,b),
c(a,b) = (1-exp(-gamma))*log(b/a)-(b^2-a^2)/2.   (6)
```

The analytic inputs are ordinary, unconditional `pi(t)~t/log t` and Mertens' finite-product formula `delta(t)log t -> exp(-gamma)`. The latter is stated in [Diamond and Pintz, equation (1.1)](https://www.numdam.org/article/JTNB_2009__21_3_523_0.pdf); the prime-counting input and its stronger error version are recorded in [NIST DLMF 27.12](https://dlmf.nist.gov/27.12). These are independent established inputs, not new results of this invocation. Their application here does not replace a prime indicator by density without an error.

Proof with uniformity: once R>=a^(-3), p>=aR implies p^3>X. All arguments p and X/p lie in a fixed compact proportional range [aR,R/a]. PNT therefore gives UNIFORM absolute error o(R/log R) in

```text
1+pi(floor(X/p))-pi(p-1)
  = (R/log R)*(1/(p/R)-p/R)+o(R/log R).
```

There are O_(a,b)(R/log R) owners. Applying PNT partial summation to the continuous function 1/u-u on [a,b] gives

```text
sum_(aR<=p<bR) Phi(X/p,p-1)
  = (log(b/a)-(b^2-a^2)/2+o(1))*T_R.             (7)
```

The unit and prime-square counts are O(R/log R), hence lower order, but were included in the exact preceding formulas. Floors and endpoint prime admission change the asymptotic only by the controlled lower-order errors, not the exact identities.

For the Euler packet mass, uniform Mertens asymptotics on the same compact range and PNT partial summation give

```text

sum_(aR<=p<bR) 1/p = (log(b/a)+o(1))/log R,
X*sum_(aR<=p<bR) delta(p-1)/p
  = (exp(-gamma)*log(b/a)+o(1))*T_R.             (8)
```

This does NOT subtract two first-order Mertens equivalents to infer a second-order difference. It estimates the exact telescoping difference through its prime-adjunction increments. Combining (7)-(8) proves (6). All little-o constants and thresholds may depend on the FIXED a,b, never on a subsequently chosen scale-dependent band.

    for p in range(2, int(n**0.5) + 1):
        if flags[p]:
            flags[p*p:n+1:p] = b"\x00" * (((n-p*p)//p) + 1)

def upper_packet(R, p):
    X = R * R - 1
    assert p < R and p**3 > X
    survivors = 1 + pi(X // p) - pi(p - 1)
    return Q(survivors) - Q(X, p) * delta(p - 1)


def band(R, den_lo, den_hi=1):

    lower = (R + den_lo - 1) // den_lo
    upper = (R + den_hi - 1) // den_hi - 1
    ps = PRIMES[bisect_left(PRIMES, lower):bisect_right(PRIMES, upper)]
