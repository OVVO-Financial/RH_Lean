$$

\frac{\mathcal E_B}{Q}=S_B-C_B, \tag{1}
$$
where the nonnegative, exactly measured correction is
$$
C_B=\frac{2}{|H|Q}\sum_{u,j}\sum_{(p,i)\in\mathcal P_B}\log p
 \sum_{t\in D(p^i)}

**Proof of the mask.** The [reduced partner][local] at every intermediate integer step $k$ is $((0,k(1+g_2)),t[p^k])$. For a central prime $g_2=1$, its bit is zero for *all* $k$. Thus the first positive hit to a distinct top occurs exactly for $1\le i<o_p$ with $t[p^i]\in TT$. This is not first entrance into $TT$ as a whole. For $o_p=1$ there is none; $i=o_p$ is a self-return; $i>o_p$ either repeats or returns to self. At a signal prime the odd bit is one and never a top. Only at the odd contributing exponent do we use the proved equality with the physical partner; no equality on even steps is asserted. Each label and each $u,t,j$ is counted once; two distinct primes sharing a residue class are still different labels. Now $\max(z-w,0)=z-\min(w,z)$ for $z,w\ge0$. Summing, the base and lift counts cancel only in the leading term, giving (1) with the *original* divisor. Width equality gives a zero hinge, not a positive atom.

### F. A real collision restriction and why it is not payment

Inside $(U,2U]$ at most one odd power of any one odd prime occurs, since successive odd powers differ by a factor at least $9$. For a fixed base and lift let $E$ be the number of actual positive directed edges.
