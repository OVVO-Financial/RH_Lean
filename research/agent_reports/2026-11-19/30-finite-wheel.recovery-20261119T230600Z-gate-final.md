## GATE1: one multiplier and its signed return

First impose a prime p in (2b/3,b). In a split the factor carrying p is <2b, hence it can only be 2p. Its other factor t is in [b,floor(X/(2p))], is odd, and is <3b/2. An odd multiple of p there would be >=3p>2b. An odd composite t in range has all prime factors <b. Conversely every squarefree such t gives precisely this split. Its sign is mu(t); no second prime in (2b/3,b) can divide t (its complementary odd factor would be >=3, or t itself would be a prime >=b). Distinct pairs are distinct integers. The excluded nonsmooth sites are exactly 2pq with genuine prime q in the same t interval; their triples are (1,-2,-1). EX3 never meets them since its three-prime endpoint has smaller prime s<=0.51b, whereas p>2b/3; its other endpoint has four prime factors.

Let C(X) be the signed smooth sum in this gate, R(X) the number of these prime replacements before deleting EX1. Write M_odd(y)=sum_{odd t<=y} mu(t). Exactly, over the above p with V=floor(X/(2p))>=b,

    C(X) = R(X) + sum_p [M_odd(V)-M_odd(b-1)].

Fix beta=15/16; merged #1119 supplies M(y)=O(y^beta) by spending a positive loss. The already-proved odd/full identity M(y)=M_odd(y)-M_odd(floor(y/2)) gives the same bound for M_odd by a geometric series. There are O(b/log b) primes p. Thus C-R=O(b^(1+beta)/log b), uniformly even before restricting X to P. This is a genuine signed improvement on the combined, *unmasked* rows; it does not make used donors available again. By ordinary PNT,

    R(X) = [A(c)+o(1)] Lb,   A(c)=(c/2)log(3c/4)-c/2+2/3  (4/3<=c<=2).

Indeed the scaled prime-pair region is 2/3<u<1, 1<=v<=c/(2u), hence area integral from 2/3 to c/2 of c/(2u)-1. Product prime counting measures converge on rectangles by PNT; sandwich this region by a finite grid, then let the mesh shrink. Monotonicity and continuity give uniformity on the compact c-range; use U, not the excluded 2b^2, at the upper end. In particular C(X) and C(U)-C(X) both have positive main terms uniformly on P. Smooth outputs beyond X reinforce the sum on this gate.

## GATE2: exterior prime supply after the old mask

Let F be the number of *unused* prime replacements from GATE1 in the whole collar through U. For all sufficiently large B and every X in P,

    C(X)-F >= Lb/100.

This allows every unused prime site inside AND outside X, arbitrary edges, and counts each at most once. It is an obstruction even to that relaxed payment, not a claim against the use of other factor families.

Proof. Orient q>=b as the anchor of the old prime-pair row, t=2p as a possible donor. In coordinates y=q/b, z=t/b the whole tagged region is 1<=y, 4/3<z<2, yz<=2, with area density 1/2 in units Lb. A good donor satisfies z>y; the remainder has limiting mass

    D=(1/2) integral_{4/3}^{sqrt(2)} (2/z-z) dz = (1/2)log(9/8)-1/18.

For y>1, z>y, yz<2 away from the boundary, q<sqrt(U), q>=b+L, and a full target cell immediately to the right of the donor cell lies below floor(U/q)+1 and strictly above q. This is exactly EX1's previous-cell rule. Cells straddling z=4/3 or a boundary can be excluded in a fixed scaled strip of width delta; L/b->0. All resulting uncounted tagged pairs there have limiting upper mass O(delta) by the same rectangle argument; send delta to zero after b tends to infinity. Equivalently use all full donor cells wholly in the good region with their full admissible successor, a union that exhausts its interior. No identification on the diagonal is needed.

In a full such cell, the target prime count is (1+o(1))L/log b; supply of distinct d*r, d=2,3,5, r>5 prime, is (31/30+o(1))L/log b; tagged 2*p supply is (1/2+o(1))L/log b. These estimates are uniform: Guth and Maynard, *New large value estimates for Dirichlet polynomials*, Corollary 1.3 (arXiv:2405.20552), gives the prime-count asymptotic for interval lengths between x^(17/30+epsilon) and x^0.99. Take, for example, epsilon=1/60; x is comparable to b/d, length comparable to L/d, so fixed factors, rounded endpoints and the log convention do not alter the estimate. The physical lists are disjoint by factorization. Each demanded prime uses one distinct supply, so total unused supply is (1/30+o(1))L/log b. Even if ALL unused supply has tag 2, at most a fraction 1/15+o(1) of the tagged cell can be unused. This counts genuine EX1 assignments in different q rows and cells only once: q is the unique prime factor >=b in these outputs. Thus F/Lb <= D+(A(2)-D)/15+o(1). This only uses compulsory depletion, not a supposed equidistribution of ranks inside a sorted bin.

Strict constants without floating-point inference: log(5/4)>2/9, log(3/2)<2/5+1/180, log(9/8)<2/17+1/7344 by the positive series 2*atanh((x-1)/(x+1)), bounding all denominators after the linear term below by 3. Hence A(5/3)>1/54, A(2)<13/180, D<1/270. The gap exceeds 1/54-(13/180+14/270)/15=83/8100>1/100 before o(1). Primes already used by EX1 cannot pay a second time. If J is the actual number of used rough sites through X, exact off-mask combined mass there is C(X)-(R(X)-J), not C(X)-R(X). Pair endpoints outside the gate keep their original signs in the full source.

## GATE3: the whole upper half and the necessary negative return

Let S_hi(X) and S_lo(X) be the smooth unique signed mass with largest prime respectively >b/2 or <=b/2. Denote by P_b the primes b/2<p<b. Define M_k(y)=sum_{t<=y,(t,k)=1}mu(t); I_k(l,h)=M_k(h)-M_k(l-1) if h>=l, and zero otherwise. Let R_d(X) count p in P_b and primes q>=b with dpq<=X, for d=2,3. There is an exact formula:

    S_hi(X) = R_2(X)+R_3(X)
       + sum_{p in P_b,d=2,3} I_{dp}(b,floor(X/(dp)))
       + 2 sum_{p in P_b} I_{6p}(ceil(b/2),floor(X/(6p))).

In a legal split the side containing p is necessarily 2p or 3p. Indicators I2,I3 for the two incidences give exclusive membership I2+I3-2I2*I3, not just their sum. For n=dp*t, t<2b, the coprimality filter gives squarefree n and sign mu(t) when t is squarefree; a nonprime t with mu(t) nonzero has all primes <b. Removing the genuine primes t>=b adds R_d. If both splits occur, n=6p*s, s>=ceil(b/2) and s<2b/3<b, with (s,6p)=1 for squarefree support, and mu(n)=-mu(s). This gives the PLUS twice-overlap correction displayed. The two splits are distinct by squarefreeness. No unique squarefree output can carry two candidate primes p,r>b/2: if they lie on one side pr>2b (b>=10); on opposite sides the factors must be 2p and 3r or 3p and 2r (the same multiplier is not squarefree). Then the factor exchange gives both splits since 2p and 2r exceed b. Thus summing over p counts each unique integer once; a twice-tagged ambiguous output cancels separately in both labels.

Here M_k(y)=sum_{a<=y, all prime factors of a divide k} M(floor(y/a)), including a=1, by Euler-factor removal (check coefficients prime-power by prime-power). The #1119 bound gives O(y^beta product_{ell|k}(1-ell^(-beta))^(-1))=O(y^beta) uniformly for k=dp,6p. Consequently, on P, with all constants before the growing cutoff,

    S_hi(X) = R_2(X)+R_3(X) + O(b^(1+beta)/log b).
    R_d(X) = [A_d(c)+o(1)] Lb,
    A_d(c) = (c/d)log(2c/d)-c/d+1/2  for c>=d/2.

Both P branches satisfy c>=d/2 and c/d<1. Integrate c/(du)-1 over 1/2<u<c/d as in GATE1. At c=5/3 the sum of areas is about 0.095333 and it increases. This main term concerns actual signed physical sites, not degree mass. The wider rough sets R_d in this comparison are NOT being assumed free of old masks. The new arithmetic is the two-multiplier exhaustion and its exact signed overlap; neither odd-prefix compression alone nor a generic factor toggle gives this restriction.

Independent check without #1119: Lee and Leong, *New explicit bounds for Mertens function and the reciprocal of the Riemann zeta-function*, arXiv:2208.06141v5, imply M(y)=O_A(y/log^A(2y)) for each fixed A>0. This remains uniform for our M_k: in the displayed smooth-a convolution split at sqrt(y); the lower range costs O_A(y/log^A(2y)) since the smooth reciprocal product is bounded, while the upper costs O(y^(3/4) product_{ell|k}(1-ell^(-1/2))^(-1)) by |M(v)|<=v. There are at most three allowed prime divisors, so this product is uniformly bounded. Thus the signed row error is O_A(b^2/log^(A+1) b); A=3 suffices for both main-term signs and the GATE2 obstruction. This classical check does not give the fixed positive power error used in the next equivalence.
