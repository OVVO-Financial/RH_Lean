#### E1. Factorization Makes The Finite Difference Exact

```text
kappa_P(n)=sum_I (-1)^|I| P(sum_(i in I)x_i).
```

radical invariance and the top-depth formula. The n=1 and j=0 cases follow

#### E3. Fixed-Degree Pole Rigidity

Write Z(s)=zeta(s), M(s)=1/Z(s), L(s)=-Z'(s)/Z(s). This M is an analytic
function, NOT the summatory Mertens function. For Re(s)>1, absolute local
uniform convergence permits finitely many derivatives and convolution:

```text
F_P(s):=sum_n kappa_P(n)n^-s
       =Z(s) sum_(j=0..D) a_j (-1)^j M^(j)(s),
H_P(s):=sum_n (kappa_P*b)(n)n^-s=F_P(s)[L(s)-Z(s)].
```

Convergence follows directly from sum |mu(n)|log^j(n)n^-sigma<infinity
for sigma>1, the zeta series and the Lambda series. These identities use
the actual full coefficients and all ordered multiplicities.

At a nontrivial zero rho of multiplicity m>=1, set u=s-rho and factor
Z(s)=u^m h(u), where h is holomorphic and h(0)!=0. Then

```text
M^(j)(s)=(-1)^j(m)_j h(0)^(-1)u^(-m-j)
                         +O(u^(-m-j+1)),
Z(s)(-1)^j M^(j)(s)=(m)_j u^-j+O(u^(-j+1)),
L(s)-Z(s)=-m/u+O(1).
```

For j=0, ZM=1 exactly. For D>=1, the highest-degree term alone contributes
u^-D to F_P; every lower j has strictly lower pole order. Multiplication
therefore gives

```text
H_P(s)=-a_D m(m)_D u^(-D-1)+O(u^-D),
```

with nonzero leading coefficient. The D=0 case follows from F_P=a_0 and
has the same formula with (m)_0=1. In particular the principal-part map
on polynomials of degree <=D is triangular of full rank D+1: no nonzero
fixed linear combination kills every pole coefficient at even one zero.
This is not an assumption about zero multiplicities or random phases.

If Q(t)=sum q_j t^j, its coefficient Dirichlet series is
sum q_j(-1)^j Z^(j)(s), holomorphic at rho. It cannot remove the pole.
Let A_PQ(x)=T_P(floor x)-sum_(n<=x)Q(log n). Under FD2's proposed bound,

```text
s integral_1^infinity A_PQ(x)x^(-s-1) dx
```

is locally uniformly convergent and holomorphic on Re(s)>beta. It equals
H_P minus the Q series initially on Re(s)>1. The meromorphic identity
theorem continues this equality; a pole at a rho in Re(s)>beta would
contradict holomorphy. This proves FD2. The entire argument is conditional
on the proposed bound, not on the existence of an off-line zero.

Taking P=t^2, Q=4 gamma^2 recovers PC.4's centered triple pole
-m^2(m+1)/(s-rho)^3. The old K2 note already supplies the corresponding
double pole for F_(t^2); convolving with b adds the extra order. This
distinction is important to the novelty and source audit.

### F. Structural Consequence

Finite-degree incidence labels are highly constrained: only up to D distinct
factors survive, the top-depth weight is forced, and the monomial case has
fixed parity sign at every admitted depth. Yet that restriction does NOT
erase the global zero-sensitive mode. Every nonzero fixed polynomial has
a nonvanishing highest pole; higher degrees add, rather than remove, that
highest obstruction. This excludes a specific algebraic cancellation
proposal, not the possibility of proving an RH-strength estimate by other
arithmetic information.

Generic mixed polynomials do not inherit the monomial sign. For example,
P(t)=t^2-2log(2)t gives kappa_P(2)=(log 2)^2>0 although omega(2)=1.
The valid statement is the exact finite-difference formula, not a universal
parity-sign slogan. Repeated powers also do not raise distinct-factor depth:
kappa_2(4)=kappa_2(2)=-(log 2)^2.
