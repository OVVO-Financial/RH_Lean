## TH2. What the signed sum pays

Let $S=\sum_I m_a$, $V=\sum_I m_a/a$, $J_R=\sum_{a,b\in I,\,ab\le T}m_am_b$. Exactly $B(m,m)=(1+c)S^2-2(R+1)SV+J_R$. For each $\epsilon>0$, $B(m,m)\ll_\epsilon R^{1+\epsilon}K$. Define $b=\max(\Theta,1/2)$. First we show $B(m,m)\ll_\epsilon R^{2b+\epsilon}$. Ordinary Mertens growth at $b$ with arbitrary positive loss and partial summation pay the first two terms. For the threshold put $y=T+1/2$, $\sigma=1/\log R$ and $D(s)=\sum_I m_a a^{-s}$. Truncated Perron gives

$$J_R=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-R^6}^{R^6} D(\sigma+it)^2 \frac{y^{\sigma+it}}{\sigma+it}\,dt+O(R^{-2}).$$

It is a square, not an absolute square. The half-integer is separated from every product by at least $1/2$, so the logarithmic distance is $\gg R^{-2}$; there are at most $R^2$ bounded weighted terms. Uniformly for $|t|\le R^6$, $D(\sigma+it)\ll_\epsilon R^{b+\epsilon}$. Details: for fixed small $\alpha>0$ and any $\nu>0$, $1/\zeta(v+iu)\ll_{\alpha,\nu}(2+|u|)^\nu$ for $v\ge b+\alpha$. For $|u|\ge4$, take the Euler branch of $\log\zeta$ on the zero- and pole-free disk centered at $2+iu$ of radius $2-b-\alpha/4$. Polynomial growth of zeta and Borel-Caratheodory give $O_\alpha(\log(2+|u|))$ on radius $2-b-\alpha/2$; the Euler product bounds it on radius $1/2$. Three circles on radius $2-b-\alpha$ give $O_\alpha((\log(2+|u|))^\kappa)$ for some $\kappa<1$. Farther right use the Euler product; on compact heights the reciprocal is regular even at 1. This uses the definition of $b$, not RH.

Truncation is $O(R\log R/R^{20})$ by the half-integer harmonic sum near $Y$ and absolute convergence away from it. Choose $\alpha,\nu$ after the desired loss; difference the prefixes and partially sum the weight $a^{-\sigma}$. Integrating the resulting bound for $D$ costs just a logarithm.
