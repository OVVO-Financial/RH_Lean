## E-G. Exact source and oscillation

**GLUE1 (PAPER_PROOF).** Let `G(x)=sum_n Lambda(n)(x/n-1)_+ - x log x + (1+gamma)x`, `Y=R^2`, `A=Y-1, B=4Y-1, C=16Y-1`, integer R>=2. Write `D_R=-G(C)+5G(B)-4G(A)` and `P_R` for the identical expression with only the k=1 occurrences of p^k in its sums; do not alter the centering. Then `D_R<0<P_R` for infinitely many R. This refutes even eventual sign preservation, not any estimate allowing an O(R) absolute error.

**GLUE2 (PAPER_PROOF of equivalence, TARGET_EQUIVALENT to ordinary RH).** RH iff `P_R>=0` for all sufficiently large integer R, equivalently iff `P_R>0` eventually. Under RH, `limsup |D_R|/R <= c=18(2+gamma-log(4 pi)) < 0.832`, and `liminf P_R/R >= 4-c`. If a zeta zero has real part beta>1/2, each of `D_R/R^(2a)` and `P_R/R^(2a)` has liminf minus infinity and limsup plus infinity for every fixed `1/2<a<beta`.

1. For unshifted y,4y,16y the deficit is `D0(y)=12y log(4)-sum_n Lambda(n)w(n/y)`, where w is zero outside [1,16], `4(1-1/u)` on [1,4] and `16/u-1` on [4,16]. This follows termwise, using the same hinge and affine centering as G. On the shifted interval the two weights are `4(1-A/n)` on (A,B] and `C/n-1` on (B,C), and the center is `C log C-5B log B+4A log A`. Prime powers k>=2 therefore return positively to P-D. PNT on the fixed rescaled interval gives the square contribution `R integral w(u) d(sqrt(u)) + o(R) = 4R+o(R)`; the shifts alter w by O(1/Y). Cubes cost O(R^(2/3)); powers k>=4 cost O(R^(1/2) log R) by Chebyshev. This does not give a cube-sized error for the PNT on squares.
2. Passing from D0(Y) to D_R costs o(1): subtracting the centers gives `3 log 4+O(1/Y)`, and the arithmetic difference is `-4 sum_[Y,4Y) Lambda(n)/n + sum_[4Y,16Y) Lambda(n)/n`; each reciprocal sum tends to log 4. Off its knots D0 has derivative `12 log 4 + 4 sum_(y,4y] Lambda(n)/n - 16 sum_(4y,16y] Lambda(n)/n = o(1)` by PNT. D0 is continuous, piecewise smooth and o(y) (PNT with the continuous compact weight). Thus for R=floor(sqrt y), `D0(y)/sqrt(y)-D_R/R=o(1)`, uniformly within that square gap; consecutive values of D_R/R differ by o(1). The normalization change uses D0=o(y), not just a pointwise oscillation claim.
3. Put `W(s)=integral w(u)u^(s-1)du=(4^s-1)(4-4^s)/(s(1-s))`, with removable endpoint values. There are no zeros in the open critical strip. On Re s>1 the Mellin transform of D0 on [1,infinity) is `12 log(4)/(s-1) + W(s) zeta'(s)/zeta(s) + E(s)`; the lower integration cutoff accounts for an entire finite correction (n<=16). At 1 the pole cancels. There are no positive real zeta zeros in (0,1), by the alternating eta series. If a zero has real part beta>a, its pole cannot cancel. An eventual bound of either sign by a constant times y^a would make the transform of `+D0+C y^a` or `-D0+C y^a` that of an eventually nonnegative function. Its convergence abscissa must be at least beta, yet the continuation is regular on the real axis above a. This contradicts Landau's lemma: after dropping an initial interval, monotone convergence of the Taylor series for the Laplace transform forces a singularity at its real finite abscissa. This proves both unbounded excursions, which transfer by step 2 and P-D=O(R).
4. Under RH Mellin inversion for the compact weight and the zeta explicit formula give `D0(y)/sqrt(y)=f(log y)+O(y^(-3/2))`, where `f(v)=sum_rho W(rho) exp(i Im(rho)v)` over all nontrivial zeros with multiplicity. In detail, shift the inverse integral of `-W zeta'/zeta` from Re s>1 to -1: the residue at 1 is the center; the residues at zeros enter D0 with plus sign. No trivial zero is crossed; on -1 the logarithmic derivative is O(log(2+|t|)). W=O((1+|t|)^(-2)) on the fixed strip at large height, and horizontal contours can be taken through heights avoiding zeros. The remaining integral is O(1/y). On the critical line the numerator has modulus at most 18. The absolutely convergent reciprocal sum is `sum_rho 1/[rho(1-rho)]=2+gamma-log(4 pi)` (subtract the logarithmic derivatives of the completed xi at 1 and 0). Thus |f|<=c. The real, uniformly convergent Fourier series f has mean zero and positive mean square: group equal ordinates before summing squared coefficients; there is no zero at ordinate 0 and W never kills a zero. If one sign had no excursions bounded away from zero arbitrarily late, the mean of that sign part, then of |f|, then of f^2 would vanish. Both signs recur. Step 2 transfers those excursions to integer R. This proves GLUE2 in both directions and GLUE1 under RH. If RH fails, symmetry supplies beta>1/2; D_R/R takes arbitrarily large values of both signs. Its consecutive changes tend to zero, so a subsequence approaches -2, while P_R/R approaches +2 there. This proves GLUE1 without assuming RH. No simplicity, linear independence of zeros, density assertion or random prime model.
