# When does reflecting the thin widths cancel?

**Finite Wheel & Incidence | November 21, 2026 | `FW-20261121T230600Z-DYADIC-FINAL`**

At common height, using every translate makes a reflected width swap cancel exactly in the native signed ASK minus MAIN. Using one translate can instead retain asymptotically the entire available logarithmic mass. The difference is governed by which pairs of actual prime powers have inverse dyadic residues. A uniform estimate below prices missing translates and height movement separately. None of these statements bounds the unchanged absolute error. **Status: paper proofs, independent review pending; no new Lean theorem or RH bound.**

## A-F. Physical carrier, theorem and proof

**DYADIC1 | Exact reflected contrast.** Let $n=2^e\ge4$, $q,d$ distinct primes, $q=3\pmod8$, $d=7\pmod{16}$, and $(-d/q)=1$ (Legendre symbol). Use the native unit host of modulus $Q=4nqd$, with the two nonsquare bits ordered $d,q$ and $H=(\mathbb Z/4n)^*/\{\pm1\}=\langle5\rangle$ in additive exponents mod $n$. Write $h(z)$ for the exponent of an odd $z$ up to sign. On $G=C_2^2\times H$, the mask is first bit zero, and the partner of $g$ is $(1,1,0)+g+(0,0,t)$. Take $T\subseteq H$, heights $a_t>0$, common levels $0<L<U$, and an integer cutoff $N$ with $a_t/w(s)\le N$ on both compared profiles at every masked site. The varying sheet is $s=(0,0,h)$, always thin; the other sheet, including its thick collar, is identical on both sides. At fixed sites $h=-h$ use $L$; on every other unordered pair $\{h,-h\}$ put $L,U$ in either order, and reflect that order to get the comparison profile. An orientation is chosen once per pair, not per occurrence.

For $z=p^i$, $i\ge1$, define $J_a(z)=(a/z-L)_+-(a/z-U)_+$. Its slope changes at $a= Lz,Uz$; the endpoints are continuous, with values $0,U-L$. The physical coefficient $k_z$ before $\log p$ is zero at $2,q$; at $d$ it is 1 for odd $i$ and zero otherwise; at every other prime it is 2 exactly for odd $i$ and both nonsquare bits, zero otherwise. Indeed the coefficient is twice the fraction of the original coset $iF_p+I_p$ with bits $(1,1)$. At $2,q$ the unramified $d$ bit is zero since $(2/d)=(q/d)=1$. At $d$ the inertia runs through its own bit, and $(d/q)=-1$. At an ordinary prime the bits are multiplied by $i$. Every nonzero row has the single dyadic coordinate $h(z)$. These statements concern the changed sheet, not vanishing of the raw ramified character sums.

Put $M_h=\sum_{t,z:\,t+h(z)=h}k_z\log p\,J_{a_t}(z)$, summing $z\le N$ with $k_z>0$, and $W=\sum_h M_h$. The maximum, over widths as above, of the **difference of the two complete real ASK minus MAIN sums over $t$**, per frame, is

$$D_T=\sum_{\{h,-h\}:h\ne-h}|M_h-M_{-h}|,\qquad W-D_T=\sum_{h=-h}M_h+2\sum_{\{h,-h\}:h\ne-h}\min(M_h,M_{-h}).$$

Proof: MAIN depends on widths only through the sum of their logarithms on the full mask, unchanged under this permutation. Its derivative constant, the principal logarithm and every unchanged hinge cancel in the comparison only. In the full prime-power ASK the difference on a pair is exactly $\pm(M_h-M_{-h})$. Each $t,p,i$ is used once at its true weight; optimizing the remaining independent pair choices proves both equalities. Two admitted occurrences $(t,z),(t',z')$ have opposite sites, including fixed sites, **iff** $zz'\equiv\pm5^{-(t+t')}\pmod{4n}$. This follows by multiplying their native unit classes. No prime has been paired with a fictitious inverse prime.

**Integral test.** Before the real weights, the evaluation matrix has one row per positive $(t,p,i)$ and one column per nonfixed pair: a signed unit in the visited column, or a zero row at a fixed site. The unweighted Gram is diagonal with the visit counts; singular values are their square roots. Selecting one row from each visited column gives a unit maximal minor on those columns; all nonzero Smith entries are 1. Repeated visits give neither new independent divisibility conditions nor an odd-prime index. This is the primitive index of this *evaluation*, not the integral field lattice underlying the widths. Its diagonal Gram is not the signed statistic.

**DYADIC2 | Complements and height movement.** At constant height $a$, full translation makes $M_h$ constant. Hence $D_H(a)=0$, $D_T(a)=D_{H\setminus T}(a)$, and $D_T(a)\le\min(|T|,n-|T|)W_1(a)$, where $W_1$ is the one-translate mass. In fact on this sheet with all translates, every permutation of one width multiset is invisible at constant height. Subtraction of the omitted profile proves the complement equality; the triangle inequality proves the bound. At varying heights, for any admitted common comparison height $a$,

$$D_T((a_t))\le\min(|T|,n-|T|)W_1(a)+2C_\psi(1+\log(U/L))\sum_{t\in T}|a_t-a|,\qquad W_1(a)\le 2C_\psi a\log(U/L),$$

where $C_\psi=4\log2$ suffices. Proof: the change of the optimum is at most the sum of absolute changes of the already assembled site masses. At intermediate height $s$, the absolute cost per unit movement for one translate is at most $2\sum_{s/U<z\le s/L}\Lambda(z)/z$. Partial summation with $\psi(y)\le C_\psi y$ gives the displayed constant, with no factor $\log N$. The Chebyshev bound follows by summing $\psi(2m)-\psi(m)\le\log\binom{2m}{m}\le2m\log2$ at dyadic $m$. Integrating $2\psi(a/v)$ over $L\le v\le U$ proves the mass bound. Thus for $n$ frames of translation and $m$ copies, even with independent pair orientations per frame, the absolute change of normalized `topExcess` divided by the actual signal $P=|T|mb\Delta/2$ is at most the right-hand side for $D_T$ times $2/(|T|b\Delta)$ when $T$ is nonempty and $b,\Delta>0$. This does not bound the frozen level.

**DYADIC3 | One translate can keep linear mass.** Take $L=1,U=2$, $T=\{0\}$, $a=x\ge2$, $N\ge\lceil x\rceil$, all other widths 1. Let $C$ count ordered integer pairs $2\le z,z'\le x$ with $zz'\equiv\pm1\pmod{4n}$. At a nonfixed orbit the deficit is at most all mass having an opposite; a fixed row is its own witness. Each $z$ is a prime power in at most one way, of weight at most $2\log x$. Therefore

$$0\le W_1-D_{\{0\}}\le 2\log x\, C,\qquad C\le2\left\lfloor\frac{x^2+1}{4n}\right\rfloor\max_{1\le k\le x^2}\tau(k).$$

Indeed $zz'=4nj\pm1$ with $j\ge1$ and at most $\tau(zz')$ ordered factorizations per product. For any fixed $0<\alpha\le1$, $n\ge x^{1+\alpha}$ gives deficit $O_\epsilon(x^{1-\alpha+\epsilon})$ for every $\epsilon>0$, uniformly in $q,d$. Here $\tau(k)\ll_\eta k^\eta$ follows by bounding each factor $v+1$ by $p^{\eta v}$ for sufficiently large $p$, with finite constants at the remaining primes. If $4n>x^2+1$ the deficit is exactly zero.

Fix $(q,d)=(3,23)$. The two nonsquare bits select a quarter of the reduced classes modulo **69**, independently of $n$. The fixed-modulus prime number theorem in progressions and partial summation give $W_1(x)\sim x\log(2)/2$: the first powers carry weight 2 and the integral of $\min(1,1/y-1)$ on $0<y\le1$ is $\log2$. Higher powers contribute $O(\sqrt{x}\log x)$ by grouping by base prime; the ramified prime contributes $O(\log x)$. Thus in the above growing-$n$ regime $D_{\{0\}}(x)\sim x\log(2)/2$. No distribution result at growing modulus is used. At $n=4,x=N=6$ the exact contrast is $2\log(5)/5$, entirely from 5; at $x=5$ it is zero and at $x=10$ the weight of 5 has saturated at $2\log5$. Six is the first integer positive-contrast height on this host. The constant-height all-translate contrast is zero in each case. The linear example is **not** an anchored family in the geometric size window, nor does it have the $Y\ge Q^{12}$ hypothesis of BASE1-3.

## G-I. What the dyadic sign split does and does not pay

**DYADIC4 | Intrinsic lengths.** For any finite $\mathbb Z_2$-module $A$ with involution $c$, set $A^\pm=\ker(1\mp c)$, $\ell=\log_2|\cdot|$. Also $d=\ell(A^+\cap A^-)-\ell(A/(A^++A^-))$, and the intersection is in $A[2]$. Finiteness of both sign parts implies finiteness of $A$ by $1-c$, even if not assumed initially. **DYADIC5 | Which discrepancy can stabilize.** Let $L_0$ be a finite free $\mathbb Z_2$-lattice with involution $c$, commuting automorphism $V$, unit $u$, and $B=(u^eV)^{2^j}-1$ of nonzero determinant. For $A=L_0/BL_0$ and $P_\pm=L_0^\pm/(1\pm c)L_0$, its intrinsic overcount is

$$d=\sum_{s=\pm}\dim\ker((\bar V_s-1)^{2^j}:P_s\to P_s).$$

Proof: there is an exact sequence $0\to P_+/BP_+\to A^+/(1+c)A\to P_-[B]\to0$. Lift a fixed class to $x$ and write $(1-c)x=By$. Injectivity of $B$ forces $cy=-y$. Changing $x$ by a lift ambiguity or a norm does not change $[y]$. If $[y]=0$, subtract $B$ times a preimage under $1-c$ to get a fixed lift. Every element of $P_-[B]$ lifts by the same equation. If a fixed lift is a norm modulo $BL_0$, subtract the norm; its remaining $B$-preimage is fixed by injectivity. This proves exactness. The $P_s$ are elementary at 2, $u=1$ there, and in characteristic two $\bar V_s^{2^j}-1=(\bar V_s-1)^{2^j}$. Finite-dimensional rank-nullity finishes the proof. The sum is bounded by $\mathrm{rank}(L_0)$, independent of twist among finite cokernels of this same lattice/action, and stabilizes once $2^j\ge\max_s\dim P_s$. It is not the cokernel length. For $L_0=\mathbb Z_2,c=V=1,u=5,e=1$, $v_2(5^{2^j}-1)=j+2$ by successive squaring, while $d=1$ throughout. An interchanged pair $A_0\oplus A_0$ has $d=0$.
