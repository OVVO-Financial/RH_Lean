### 2. Actual divisibility on the matched face | INDEX1-3

**INDEX1 (integer and rational maps).** Put $k=|P|\ge1$, $D_0=\prod_{q\in P}q$, $W=\log D_0$. The bare row is exactly $g_p=p^2/D_0$ in positive rationals: $\sum w_p\log g_p=2\sum w_p^2-W^2$. This retains the mean and every cross-triple pair. For $k\ge3$ its reduced denominator $D_0/p$ exceeds its numerator $p$, so its rational logarithmic height is $W-w_p$. The ordered return restores $D_0$ inside each row ($+W^2$ after weighting), leaving the integer $p^2$. All these rationals and integers are 2-adic units, not divisible by 2. By contrast, the exponent matrix $2I-J$ doubles centered vectors, has principal eigenvalue $2-k$, and for $k\ge2$ has Smith entries $1,2$ (the latter repeated $k-2$ times), followed by $2|k-2|$, with zero last entry at $k=2$. Indeed every $j$-minor is divisible by $2^{j-1}$ by column differences; for $j<k$, rows $1,\dots,j$ and columns $2,\dots,j+1$ attain it up to sign. The determinant is $(2-k)2^{k-1}$. For $k=1$ the entry is 1. This genuine **coefficient** index is not the valuation of the evaluated row.

An honest different map, with an explicit price, is multiplication by $p^2-a$ on $\mathbb Z_2$ at an integer anchor $a$. When nonzero its index is $2^{c_p}$, $c_p=v_2(|p^2-a|)$. Write $o_p=|p^2-a|/2^{c_p}$. The exact real row is $2w_p^2=w_p[c_p\log2+\log o_p+\log(p^2/|p^2-a|)]$. The last term is signed; do not discard the odd part or movement. A zero difference is a singular map, not a finite-index dividend. This tests a specified native scalar comparison, **not** an identification with an external anchored lattice.

**INDEX2 (sharp full-band mean).** At $a=1$ put $V=\log2\sum w_pc_p$. For the full band, as $R\to\infty$,

$$\frac{\sum w_pc_p}{\sum w_p}\longrightarrow4,\qquad V\sim R\log2,\qquad \frac{V}{D_P}\sim\frac{2\log2}{\log R}.$$

Proof. All primes here are odd; $c_p\ge3$. For $j\ge3$, $c_p\ge j$ iff $p\equiv\pm1\pmod {2^{j-1}}$: one of $p-1,p+1$ has valuation exactly one. For larger $q$ the two progressions in the actual band contain at most $R/2^j+2$ integers, and $2^{c_p}\le2R$. The summed large-modulus log mass is $O(\sqrt R\log R+\log^2R)$. ### 3. Full endpoint lattice and overlap | INDEX4-5

**INDEX4 (saturation with history).** Write $L(n)=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)$, $X_r=r^2-1$, $b_r=L(X_{r+1})/L(X_r)$. For every $H\ge3$ the subgroup generated by $L(X_H),\ldots,L(X_{2H})$ in the positive rationals is saturated: if $y^d$ belongs for rational $y>0$, $d\ge1$, then $y$ belongs. Equivalently every nonzero Smith factor of the prime-valuation matrix is 1. No assumption of a prime between successive squares or of full row rank.

Proof. $v_p(L(n))$ counts the powers $p^j\le n$, $j\ge1$. Subtract consecutive rows, retaining the first anchor. Row $r$ of the increment matrix contains one at $p$ iff a power lies in $[r^2,(r+1)^2)$; the ratio of endpoints is less than 2 for $r\ge3$, so no repeated base in one row. On the union $[H^2,4H^2)$, 2 has exactly two powers, 3 at most two, and every other base at most one. Thus at most two columns meet two rows. Their undirected edges form a bipartite graph, even if parallel. Change row signs by a two-coloring. Every column has at most one positive and one negative unit. In any square minor, expand on a column of size at most one; if all columns have two entries their column sums vanish and the determinant is zero. Induction proves total unimodularity of **the increments**. For the anchor choose $2H<q<4H\le H^2$ by Bertrand for $H\ge4$ (or $q=7$ for $H=3$); $q$ is prime, its anchor exponent is 1 and there are no later powers in the window. Its column eliminates every other anchor entry by integral column operations. The resulting direct sum of a unit and the increment matrix has only unit nonzero Smith factors. Transposition gives exactly the stated multiplicative subgroup; omitted primes outside the matrix cannot divide a rational root of a supported product. The raw undifferenced matrix is **not** asserted totally unimodular. At $H=4$ the columns $11,17,29,37,53$ already give an identity after differencing.
