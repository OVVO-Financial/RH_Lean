### 1. Source and scope

The recurrence is $E(X)\log^2X=S(X)\log X-\sum_{a\le X}\Lambda(a)S(t_a)+\sum_{n\le X}K_2(n)E(t_n)-D$, where $D=\sum\Lambda(a)E(t_a)\log(X/(a t_a))$ and $S$ is the original signed first remainder. ### 2. Actual divisibility on the matched face | INDEX1-3

**INDEX1 (integer and rational maps).** Put $k=|P|\ge1$, $D_0=\prod_{q\in P}q$, $W=\log D_0$. The bare row is exactly $g_p=p^2/D_0$ in positive rationals: $\sum w_p\log g_p=2\sum w_p^2-W^2$. This retains the mean and every cross-triple pair. For $k\ge3$ its reduced denominator $D_0/p$ exceeds its numerator $p$, so its rational logarithmic height is $W-w_p$. The ordered return restores $D_0$ inside each row ($+W^2$ after weighting), leaving the integer $p^2$. All these rationals and integers are 2-adic units, not divisible by 2. By contrast, the exponent matrix $2I-J$ doubles centered vectors, has principal eigenvalue $2-k$, and for $k\ge2$ has Smith entries $1,2$ (the latter repeated $k-2$ times), followed by $2|k-2|$, with zero last entry at $k=2$. Indeed every $j$-minor is divisible by $2^{j-1}$ by column differences; for $j<k$, rows $1,\dots,j$ and columns $2,\dots,j+1$ attain it up to sign. The determinant is $(2-k)2^{k-1}$. For $k=1$ the entry is 1. This genuine **coefficient** index is not the valuation of the evaluated row.

An honest different map, with an explicit price, is multiplication by $p^2-a$ on $\mathbb Z_2$ at an integer anchor $a$. When nonzero its index is $2^{c_p}$, $c_p=v_2(|p^2-a|)$. Write $o_p=|p^2-a|/2^{c_p}$. The exact real row is $2w_p^2=w_p[c_p\log2+\log o_p+\log(p^2/|p^2-a|)]$. The last term is signed; do not discard the odd part or movement. A zero difference is a singular map, not a finite-index dividend. This tests a specified native scalar comparison, **not** an identification with an external anchored lattice.

**INDEX2 (sharp full-band mean).** At $a=1$ put $V=\log2\sum w_pc_p$. For the full band, as $R\to\infty$,

$$\frac{\sum w_pc_p}{\sum w_p}\longrightarrow4,\qquad V\sim R\log2,\qquad \frac{V}{D_P}\sim\frac{2\log2}{\log R}.$$

Proof. All primes here are odd; $c_p\ge3$. For $j\ge3$, $c_p\ge j$ iff $p\equiv\pm1\pmod {2^{j-1}}$: one of $p-1,p+1$ has valuation exactly one. For fixed $j\ge4$ the log-prime fraction tends to $2^{3-j}$ by the fixed-modulus prime number theorem in progressions. There is no interchange of an uncontrolled tail. Put $q=2^{j-1}$. For $q\le\sqrt R$, Brun-Titchmarsh gives log mass $O(R/q)$ over the two reduced classes (bound by primes through $R$). For larger $q$ the two progressions in the actual band contain at most $R/2^j+2$ integers, and $2^{c_p}\le2R$. The summed large-modulus log mass is $O(\sqrt R\log R+\log^2R)$. Thus the normalized tail past fixed $J$ is $O(2^{-J})+o(1)$. The mean is $3+\sum_{j\ge4}2^{3-j}=4$. Finally $W\sim R/4$ and $w_p=\log R+O(1)$ uniformly. The movement at anchor 1 totals $O(1/R)$ since $\log(p^2/(p^2-1))=O(R^{-2})$. Most of the matched height is the odd part, not an unpriced small correction. Inputs: Montgomery and Vaughan, *The large sieve*, Mathematika 20 (1973), Thm. 2, DOI 10.1112/S0025579300004708; fixed-modulus PNT, e.g. Thorner-Zaman, arXiv:2108.10878v2, Cor. 1.4. No assertion that primes are random.

**INDEX3 (adaptive anchor versus multiplicity).** For any $k\ge1$ band primes choose arbitrary integer anchors $a_p$ from a list of $s\ge1$ distinct values, with $p^2\ne a_p$. Put $b_0=\lfloor\log_2(R^2+\max|a|)\rfloor$, $J=\max(3,\lceil\log_2(sR/k)\rceil)$. Uniformly, without any prime-distribution theorem,

$$\sum_{p\in P}c_p\le k(J+1)+2s b_0.$$

For each anchor and $j\ge3$, either there is no odd square root or, relative to one root $u$, all odd solutions are $p\equiv\pm u\pmod{2^{j-1}}$. This follows by applying the same valuation argument to $(p-u)(p+u)$, including equality. Thus at most $s(R/2^j+2)$ primes pass level $j$, regardless of how the list and assignments were selected. In particular fix $A>0$ and $0<\eta<1/2$. If $|a|\le R^A$ and $\log2\sum w_pc_p\ge\eta D_P$, then $k\ll_{A,\eta}sR^{1-2\eta}$. This is a test of **this** credit model, not a requirement of Alpoge's theorem. The restriction on the number of anchors is necessary: choose $a_p=p^2-2^{\lfloor\log_2R\rfloor}$ separately. ### 3. Full endpoint lattice and overlap | INDEX4-5

**INDEX4 (saturation with history).** Write $L(n)=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)$, $X_r=r^2-1$, $b_r=L(X_{r+1})/L(X_r)$. For every $H\ge3$ the subgroup generated by $L(X_H),\ldots,L(X_{2H})$ in the positive rationals is saturated: if $y^d$ belongs for rational $y>0$, $d\ge1$, then $y$ belongs. Equivalently every nonzero Smith factor of the prime-valuation matrix is 1. No assumption of a prime between successive squares or of full row rank.

Proof. $v_p(L(n))$ counts the powers $p^j\le n$, $j\ge1$. Subtract consecutive rows, retaining the first anchor. Row $r$ of the increment matrix contains one at $p$ iff a power lies in $[r^2,(r+1)^2)$; the ratio of endpoints is less than 2 for $r\ge3$, so no repeated base in one row. On the union $[H^2,4H^2)$, 2 has exactly two powers, 3 at most two, and every other base at most one. Thus at most two columns meet two rows. Their undirected edges form a bipartite graph, even if parallel. Change row signs by a two-coloring. Every column has at most one positive and one negative unit. In any square minor, expand on a column of size at most one; if all columns have two entries their column sums vanish and the determinant is zero. Induction proves total unimodularity of **the increments**. For the anchor choose $2H<q<4H\le H^2$ by Bertrand for $H\ge4$ (or $q=7$ for $H=3$); $q$ is prime, its anchor exponent is 1 and there are no later powers in the window. Its column eliminates every other anchor entry by integral column operations. The resulting direct sum of a unit and the increment matrix has only unit nonzero Smith factors. Transposition gives exactly the stated multiplicative subgroup; omitted primes outside the matrix cannot divide a rational root of a supported product. The raw undifferenced matrix is **not** asserted totally unimodular. At $H=4$ the columns $11,17,29,37,53$ already give an identity after differencing. At $r=2$, $b_2=140$ contains $2^2$, refuting the unqualified increment claim.

**INDEX5 (sharp repair in the signed moment).** In this dyadic window keep each base in its first increment and remove it from any later one, giving pairwise coprime squarefree $u_r$. This is a local allocation starting at $H$, **not** coprimality with the anchor. For every $H\le R\le2H$,

$$L(X_R)=L(X_H)\prod_{H\le r<R}u_r\ d_R,\qquad d_R\in\{1,2,3,6\}.$$

There is one removed factor per second occurrence; in the full window $d_{2H}$ is 2 or 6. The endpoint conventions put $p^2$ in row $p$, never row $p-1$. Define $Z_R=E(X_H)+\sum_{H\le r<R}(\log u_r-(2r+1))$. Since $\psi(n)=\log L(n)$, the **entire** original signed endpoint is $E(X_R)=Z_R+\log d_R$. This preserves its historical anchor, deterministic mean and all later interactions. For $U(Y)=\sum_{H\le R<2H}(Y_R)_+^2$,

$$0\le\sqrt{U(E)}-\sqrt{U(Z)}\le\sqrt H\log6,\qquad 0\le U(E)-U(Z)\le 2\log6\sum_{H\le R<2H}E(X_R)_+.$$

These follow from monotonicity and 1-Lipschitzness of positive part; the second inequality includes the mixed term, not just the squares of the removed corrections. Ordinary PNT alone gives $o(H^3)$ for this difference. Using the repository's #1119 baseline gives the explicit $O(H^{25/9}\log(2H))$ for this **difference only**. To see it without an additional zero-density premise, spend the fixed positive #1119 margin to use $M(t)=O(t^{7/8})$. For $x\ge2$ split $\psi(x)=\sum_{dn\le x}\mu(d)\log n$ at $d\le y=x^{8/9}$. In the small part use $\log(\lfloor t\rfloor!)=t\log t-t+O(\log(2t))$ and extend the two sums $\sum\mu(d)/d=0$, $\sum\mu(d)\log d/d=-1$ to infinity. These values follow by Abel limiting $1/\zeta(s)$ and its derivative at 1; partial summation with the stated Mertens bound controls both tails. The main term is $x$, and the total error is $O((y+xy^{-1/8})\log(2x))$. In the large part sum $\log n[M(x/n)-M(y)]$ over $n\le x/y$; the same $O(xy^{-1/8}\log(2x))$ follows. Hence $E(x)=O(x^{8/9}\log(2x))$ at integers. Constants are independent of $H$. Neither estimate is a bound for $U(Z)$, and making the increments coprime does not center their sizes.
