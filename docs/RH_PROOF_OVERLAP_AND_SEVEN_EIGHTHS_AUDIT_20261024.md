## 1. Four different kinds of arrows, never interchangeable

- **EXACT IDENTITY:** equal finite scalars on the same carrier/quantifiers. A coordinate change transfers the value but does not bound it.
- **EXACT WEIGHTED PROJECTION:** e.g. discrete summation/Cesàro of pointwise discrepancies. It loses pointwise information; its sign or norm need not recover a pointwise wall.
- **QUANTIFIED IMPLICATION:** a specific input estimate yields RH or another estimate, but the reverse hypothesis may be unproved. Mere shared denominators or signs are not an iff.
- **INDEPENDENT ARITHMETIC GAIN:** a new source-level uniform bound, valid original coefficients and every complementary term, and a certified exponent/coefficient/recurrence improvement. This alone deserves progress credit beyond an equivalent endpoint.

The repo has three **different** `S=A-T` carriers: unsigned least-prime square-block candidate minus composite removals; signed `M(R²-1)=A_mu(R)-T_mu(R)`; and a separately normalized Hecke/ideal transform. The [cross-coordinate contract](CROSS_COORDINATE_LOW_QUOTIENT_RESEARCH_2026_10_17.md#1-three-different-sa-t-equations-keep-their-carriers-separate) explicitly forbids conflating their physical sites, coefficients, source masks or NNS covariance. The textual symbol `S=A-T` cannot be a two-way proof arrow without an occurrence-preserving source theorem.

### Two decisive baseline power checks

**(a) The square-prefix continuous drift does not disappear.** Use PNT's known `sum mu(n)/n=0` and partial summation of the 7/8 Mertens estimate to obtain

\[
m(B)=\sum_{1\le n<B}\frac{\mu(n)}n
     =O_\epsilon(B^{-1/8+\epsilon}).
\]

The fixed-square drift in #1019, with A and B comparable, is

\[
(B^2-A^2)m(A)^2=O_\epsilon(B^{7/4+2\epsilon})
\quad(\text{with }B\asymp A).
\]

In terms of `X=B²`, this is exactly `O(X^(7/8+epsilon))`. Merely knowing 7/8 thus **reproduces its own current scale** rather than "killing the continuous drift". A hypothetical kth-degree product `B^k m(B)^k` would similarly retain `X^(7/8+epsilon)` for `X=B^k` unless additional structural cancellation is proved. This is a power audit of a proposed term, **not** a claim that the genuine kth-order Newton source equals that product.

**(b) The q² daughter also retains the parent 7/8 exponent.** If `R²\asymp X`, then applying the pointwise imported 7/8 bound separately to every daughter at `X/q²` gives

\[
\sum_{q²<R} M(\lfloor (R²-1)/q²\rfloor)^2
\ll_\epsilon R^{7/2+\epsilon}
\sum_{q\text{ prime}}q^{-7/2-\epsilon}
=O_\epsilon(R^{7/2+\epsilon}).
\]

This is the *existing* upper scale for `M(R²)^2`, not CORR-4's new factor-four signed inequality. A sharper recursive coefficient needs actual source coupling, not termwise Mertens growth.
